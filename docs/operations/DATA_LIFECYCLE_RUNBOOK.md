## Closure, erasure and receipts

Before customer or merchant closure can commit, the application uploads and verifies an independently encrypted closure intent, keyed by the random receipt UUID. If the archive is unavailable or configuration is missing, the transaction rolls back and the action reports failure; it must not return a false closed receipt. The customer may retry or contact the merchant/operator for assistance. Do not treat a failed request as completed closure.

Customer closure immediately retires browser and scan credentials, removes contact/verification/export proof material, suppresses pending communication and prevents new loyalty operations. It queues Wallet revocation and returns a random deletion receipt. The remaining closed record graph is restricted pending erasure; closing the card alone is not evidence of a finished purge.

A card without remaining Wallet work is eligible for complete graph deletion, including ledger/redemption/purchase/consent/security history, within 24 hours. Allow at most seven days for unresolved Wallet work absent a specific valid hold. At that deadline the local purge records `remote_revocation_incomplete` if necessary. This is not a successful remote revocation claim: a provider acknowledgment cannot prove an offline device refreshed or removed its saved pass. Server credentials stay invalid immediately, including during provider/budget outages. Application hard caps can also block emergency Wallet requests; see [service limits](SERVICE_LIMITS.md).

Merchant deletion requires an active owner session created by sign-in no more than ten minutes earlier, an unexpired session, the exact typed business name and explicit confirmation. A manager/cashier cannot initiate it. Closure atomically stops enrollment, earning/redemption, queued messages, provider issuance and staff access/sessions. It does not cancel a genuine external payment contract automatically. Complete tenant erasure is due within seven days, subject to a specific hold and the accounting classification below. The receipt reports pending/completed time, policy and remote outcome/exception without retaining the deleted subject's identity. Receipt access is by its random UUID; keep it private.

Do not restore customer records to answer an access request or undo a deletion. A mistaken closure requires Privacy Lead review; there is no automatic reversal that revives a retired bearer. No authorized purge is reversible by a down migration.

## Authority and legal/accounting gates

Migration 0031 creates four **NOLOGIN** roles. `loynara_purge_owner` owns narrow security-definer functions with fixed `search_path`; no application/operator login is made a member of that owner role. `loynara_runtime` receives ordinary application privileges and only safe retention metadata/health access. `loynara_maintenance` can invoke reviewed hold/classification/candidate/pruning/purge entry points, without arbitrary table DML. `loynara_restore` can invoke isolated replay, without ordinary application or maintenance authority. Use separate restricted login identities. Runtime and maintenance/restore membership must never overlap. Do not grant superuser, CREATEROLE, BYPASSRLS or ownership to runtime/scheduled jobs.

Ledger and other immutable-history guards still reject ordinary updates/deletes. Only the private function-owner path, for an eligible closed subject and respecting holds, allows the narrow DELETE exception. Never disable triggers, grant the application the purge-owner role, grant all tables/functions indiscriminately or use a caller-set flag as deletion authorization. Provisioning and restore ownership repair use the trusted [provisioning SQL](../../scripts/provision-retention-roles.sql) under the separately authorized database administrator.

**Accounting classification remains a case-specific decision.** Owner/accountant must assess whether actual statutory invoices/records exist; the general policy approval did not decide that fact for a merchant. An unclassified closed tenant fails closed, records an accounting-classification exception and alerts. Through a private parameterized operator session with maintenance authority, the reviewed interfaces are:

```sql
SELECT public.loynara_classify_tenant_retention($1::uuid,$2::text,$3::text,$4::text);
SELECT public.loynara_set_retention_hold($1::uuid,$2::uuid,$3::text[],$4::text,$5::text,$6::text,$7::timestamptz,$8::text);
SELECT public.loynara_release_retention_hold($1::uuid);
```

Bind classification as `no_statutory_records` only after the accountant's assessment, or `archive_verified` only after a separately controlled statutory archive has been verified; its reference is mandatory. Record the responsible classifier privately. These signatures are review instructions, not executable requests with invented subjects or approvals. A wrong classification can erase records: require documented case authorization before invoking it.

A hold binds an exact tenant and optional member, allowed categories (`membership`, `ledger`, `redemptions`, `purchases`, `consent`, `security`, `tenant`), legal basis, specific reason, custodian, review time and release condition. Review time must be in the future and within 30 days; reaching it alerts and does **not** release the hold. A tenant-level hold blocks that tenant; a member hold blocks that member and whole-tenant deletion. Do not create blanket speculative holds.

Complete graph erasure is atomic and respects applicable holds. Category-aware retention preserves the minimum closed records needed for the held categories and referential integrity; unrelated closed-card data still follows its approved deadline. Financial history and its required membership/program links are retained only where that category or unresolved statutory classification requires them. Ordinary activity and marketing remain stopped. Category holds do not preserve contact/authentication credentials, raw queued messages or other data removed at closure. If a lawful case requires preserving such material, the Privacy Lead must arrange a proportionate restricted archive before closure; the ordinary app is not that archive. Tell the requester the specific retained categories/basis, subject to lawful restrictions; internal reason/custodian details are not part of public export.

## Encrypted erasure archive and scheduled work

Closure intents make immediate credential revocation durable across recovery, including the interval before the first purge. Before first activation, the custodian must capture every pre-existing closed subject from the 0024-era source into the initialized closure archive and reconcile that inventory, following production step 5. Legacy closures are not automatically backfilled by an additive migration. Unreconciled historical obligations prevent recovery signoff. Each purge must first upload and verify an encrypted prepared intent in a separately protected offsite namespace. Only then may the narrow database purge commit, followed by an encrypted completion record. A failure between commit and completion is an unresolved prepared intent, retained and replayed conservatively. Storage refusal, missing namespace, encryption failure or database refusal stops the purge; never bypass the archive to clear a backlog.

The archive stores minimum scope/subject UUID, cutoff, receipt/intent/policy/namespace identity and closure/purge outcome, without card tokens, email or record contents. Protect it as pseudonymous data. Keep the age private identity outside the application/scheduled runtime with primary and alternate custodians. The web/worker closure path gets only the public encryption recipient and closure-only writer credentials. The maintenance job needs separate purge-archive authority plus closure-writer configuration for inactivity closure. Neither runtime receives the private identity. Initialize and verify both namespaces before deploying code that requires closure durability. Keep both namespaces independent from the backup `loynara/` prefix and its 14-day lifecycle. The closure archive must use a **different bucket** from the purge archive; provider credentials must be scoped to the correct bucket, not assumed to be restricted by a key prefix. A compromised runtime must never be able to forge prepared/completed purge records and cause financial-data erasure on restore. The closure reader accepts only closure records; the purge reader accepts only prepared/completed purge records. Do not let backup lifecycle rules delete either archive prematurely.

Custodian-only initialization, after injecting both separately scoped configurations described below, verifying two new protected namespaces and approving storage configuration:

```sh
ERASURE_INITIALIZE_CONFIRMED=new-protected-namespace python3 scripts/erasure_manifest.py initialize
ERASURE_INITIALIZE_CONFIRMED=new-protected-namespace python3 scripts/erasure_manifest.py initialize-closures
python3 scripts/erasure_manifest.py check-namespace
```

For the maintenance purge archive, inject actual restricted settings secretly: `ERASURE_S3_ENDPOINT` (the approved EU private endpoint), `ERASURE_S3_BUCKET`, `ERASURE_S3_PREFIX`, `ERASURE_NAMESPACE_ID` (UUID), `ERASURE_AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID`, `ERASURE_AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`, and `ERASURE_AGE_RECIPIENT`. For the runtime closure archive use `ERASURE_CLOSURE_S3_ENDPOINT`, `ERASURE_CLOSURE_S3_BUCKET`, `ERASURE_CLOSURE_S3_PREFIX`, `ERASURE_CLOSURE_NAMESPACE_ID`, `ERASURE_CLOSURE_AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID` and `ERASURE_CLOSURE_AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`, the closure endpoint and public age recipient; the bucket must differ from `ERASURE_S3_BUCKET`. Runtime must receive the nonsecret `ERASURE_S3_BUCKET` name so distinct-bucket validation can run; it must not receive the purge bucket credentials. The custodian initializes the closure namespace separately as shown above, before deploy. Its public recipient can be set as `ERASURE_CLOSURE_AGE_RECIPIENT` or fall back to `ERASURE_AGE_RECIPIENT`. After initialization, `check-namespace`, `verify`, `replay` and custodian `prune` require both stores. The tools validate namespace, recipient binding and object metadata. Initialization must not be an automatic fallback in a purge job. Restrict object permissions so the scheduled writer cannot remove the archive; give listing/decryption/pruning only to the separately reviewed custodian identities.

The operator-only bounded scheduled command is:

```sh
npm run retention:prune
```

Or, as its own always-on service from the same image instead of a cron entry, `npm run retention:schedule` (one `retention:prune` run every five minutes in a child process; never overlapping; TERM, then KILL, at 270 seconds). It needs the same configuration and must not run inside web or worker.

Required configuration: `ERASURE_POLICY_APPROVED=2026-10-15`, runtime `DATABASE_URL`, separate maintenance `PGHOST`, `PGPORT`, `PGDATABASE`, `PGUSER` and private credential injection, both independently scoped archive configurations above (runtime closure and maintenance purge), `PRODUCTION_ENVIRONMENT=loynara-production` and `RETENTION_HEARTBEAT_URL`. The maintenance target must be the reviewed private `*.railway.internal` database; local allowances are only for owned isolated synthetic fixtures. Do not include migration/admin credentials or private age identities in this service. Provision the runtime login's ordinary DML and the maintenance login's distinct group membership before enabling it.

Propose a **five-minute schedule**, separately approved before production activation. Before mutation, the job compares the actual database name, server address and server port returned by both runtime and maintenance connections. Missing/different identities fail closed; use matching direct private connections. A run takes a shared advisory lock; overlap skips without a success heartbeat. Each batch reviews up to 50 zero-balance cards and 50 nonzero review flags/cleanup rows, prunes at most 100 eligible rows per table/category, and fetches at most 50 total card/tenant candidates. Each card closes in its own transaction, so a slow/failing archive does not lock every candidate or roll back an earlier committed closure.

The cycle drains repeated bounded batches until no work remains, subject to a 240-second work deadline. Positive pruning counts cause another pass even below the batch size, because one category can unblock another. A purge child receives 45 seconds before TERM, with 2 seconds for tracked process-group cleanup before KILL; do not start it without 47 seconds remaining. Inactivity uses the shared cycle deadline and requires 62 seconds before another closure archive. A failed step, unknown measurement, deadline exhaustion or undrained backlog fails the run and sends no success heartbeat. Row limits alone are not a deadline guarantee. Measure capacity and adjust only through a reviewed change. Never let category retention go unprocessed for a day; 24-hour deletion deadlines need shorter detection/recovery.

Use a **separate** retention success heartbeat monitor. It is emitted only after a completed non-skipped cycle; a failed child/archive/prune step or unreachable monitor fails the run and sends no success heartbeat. Its alert destination/grace must detect missing five-minute runs early enough to act before deadlines. Web uptime, worker, operations and backup freshness monitors remain independent.

The operations check alerts on any overdue card/tenant erasure, due hold review, incomplete remote revocation or unclassified closed tenant, in addition to queue/provider/budget failures. Missing database/heartbeat means unknown health. On failure: stop discretionary lifecycle/configuration changes, retain already-invalid credentials, assign the Custodian/Privacy Lead, fix the actual archive/role/provider issue and reconcile prepared intents before a reviewed rerun. Do not mark overdue work successful or raise caps silently.

Manifest expiry is custodian work, **not** the ordinary scheduled prune. `python3 scripts/erasure_manifest.py prune` requires a verified current archive, separate backup-inventory credentials, `ERASURE_PRUNE_CONFIRMED=completed-30-days-and-other-backups-expired` and an actual UTC `ERASURE_OTHER_BACKUPS_EXPIRED_THROUGH` checkpoint. Completed evidence must be at least 30 days old and every older affected backup must have expired/deleted. The tool checks the private backup inventory; an unresolved prepared intent is never aged away. Do not set the confirmation merely because an object lifecycle is configured.

## Restoring without resurrecting deleted subjects

Follow [backup/restore](BACKUP_RESTORE.md). Keep the empty isolated restore target quarantined: no public route, web, worker, scheduler or provider credentials. Pause source writers, closures/purges and hold changes long enough to capture current independent closure and purge archive checkpoints plus the current hold register. Restrict both the snapshot and evidence; never test a real backup in the development checkout.

1. Restore/validate the exact backup-time schema and complete migration hashes/timestamps against its trusted release. A backup through 0024 requires that historical release's manifest; never accept a prefix of a newer manifest silently.
2. Before serving the target, apply the reviewed current additive migrations through 0027 from a trusted current recovery artifact under isolated administrator authority. The ordinary restore wrapper deliberately does not invent upgrades; an old 0024 target refuses current provisioning until this separate quarantined migration step is complete. Preserve the restored data and migration history; no seeds or down migrations.
3. Run `psql -X -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1 -f scripts/provision-retention-roles.sql` from that current artifact under the isolated Custodian/administrator identity. Recheck fixed function ownership/search paths, NOLOGIN groups and grants. Do not inherit function ACL/ownership from an untrusted dump. Runtime/maintenance logins remain separate and withheld from traffic.
4. Produce a current private `RESTORE_CURRENT_HOLDS_FILE` (mode 0400/0600, owned by the custodian, at most 1 MiB/1000 holds) containing exactly `schema_version:1`, current UTC `generated_at`, and `holds`. Each hold has `tenant_uuid`, nullable `member_uuid`, `categories`, `basis`, `reason`, `custodian`, `review_at`, `release_condition`. Include an explicitly verified empty list if no holds exist. Checkpoint age must be under 15 minutes; due/expired hold reviews require fresh custodian action rather than silently dropping the hold.
5. Supply both decryption identities privately (`BACKUP_AGE_IDENTITY_FILE` and `ERASURE_AGE_IDENTITY_FILE`, plus `ERASURE_CLOSURE_AGE_IDENTITY_FILE` if a distinct closure key is used; otherwise it falls back to the erasure identity) only to the custodian restore environment. Explicitly set all outbound enable flags to `false`, remove `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY`, and inject `RESTORE_TARGET_CONFIRMED=isolated`, `RESTORE_ERASURE_CHECKPOINT_CONFIRMED=current-writers-paused`, `RESTORE_TRANSPORTS_CONFIRMED=disabled`, `RESTORE_LEGAL_HOLDS_CONFIRMED=current`, the distinct target/source identities and approved policy. Confirmation strings attest actual conditions; they do not create isolation.
6. Run `python3 scripts/erasure_manifest.py verify-holds`, then `python3 scripts/erasure_manifest.py replay` in that confirmed target context. The current tool first reapplies current holds using the isolated administrator connection, then invokes erasure replay with `SET ROLE loynara_restore`. A restore-only login cannot perform provisioning/hold repair. The source of authority is both trusted current independent archive namespaces and the current hold checkpoint, never the backup's older manifest. Current closure intents replay through the restore-only closure interface before traffic: retire restored bearers, suppress sends, remove contact/proof material, queue Wallet revocation and, for a tenant, revoke staff/tag/service access. Financial history remains restricted until its approved purge eligibility. Prepared/completed erasures also replay before traffic; missing/unverifiable evidence refuses recovery.
7. Revalidate current journal hashes, balances, link/tenant constraints, held-data restrictions and receipt/outcome state **after** replay. Only a separately approved cutover may restore web/worker access. Record actual checks and downtime/data-loss interval. A schema-restoration PASS alone is not permission to serve old customer data.

Application rollback must preserve these deletion protections, the current erasure archive and approved policy. Historical code that bypasses closure/role checks is incompatible even if its schema queries run. Use a reviewed compatible repair release when necessary; no down migration or old deployment may reactivate erased subjects.
